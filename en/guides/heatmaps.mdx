Heatmaps are one of the most powerful ways to read spatial data at a glance. Rather than showing individual points, a heatmap reveals where things *concentrate* — surfacing hotspots, deserts, and gradients that would be invisible in a raw scatter plot. MapX generates heatmaps from any dataset through a single plain-English prompt, with no layer configuration, color ramp selection, or kernel tuning required.

## Types of Heatmaps in MapX

### Density Heatmaps

A density heatmap shows **where points cluster together**, regardless of any attribute value. Intensity is determined purely by how many points fall within a given radius. Use density heatmaps when you want to answer questions like:

- *Where do incidents occur most frequently?*
- *Which neighborhoods have the highest concentration of listings?*
- *Where is foot traffic heaviest across my store network?*

Every point contributes equally to the density surface — a single data row equals one unit of intensity.

### Weighted Heatmaps

A weighted heatmap uses **a numeric column to modulate intensity**. Points in a dense cluster contribute more heat if their attribute values are high. Use weighted heatmaps when the *magnitude* of an attribute matters, not just its presence:

- *Which store locations generate the most revenue?*
- *Where is property value highest per square meter?*
- *Which service areas have the greatest unmet demand?*

When you specify a column in your prompt, MapX automatically switches to weighted mode.

## Creating a Heatmap

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload your dataset">
    Upload a CSV, GeoJSON, or Excel file containing your point data. MapX will auto-detect location columns (`lat`/`lng`, address strings, or geometry fields) and render the raw points on the map.

    Your dataset can be as small as a few dozen rows or as large as hundreds of thousands of points — MapX's WebGL renderer handles either without performance loss.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask for the heatmap">
    Type your request in the AI chat. For a **density heatmap**, simply describe what you want visualized:

    ```text
    Show a heatmap of incident frequency across the city
    ```

    For a **weighted heatmap**, specify the column to use as intensity:

    ```text
    Create a heatmap weighted by revenue for each store location
    ```

    ```text
    Show a heatmap of property values using the price_per_sqm column
    ```

    MapX detects the intent, selects the appropriate heatmap mode, and renders the layer immediately.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Adjust radius and intensity">
    Fine-tune the visual appearance with follow-up prompts:

    ```text
    Increase the heatmap radius to 2km
    ```

    ```text
    Reduce the blur radius — I want to see tighter, more distinct hotspots
    ```

    ```text
    Increase the intensity — the heatmap looks too faint
    ```

    You can also ask for specific color ramps: *"Use a blue-to-red color scale"* or *"Switch to a purple gradient."*
  </Step>

  <Step title="Switch to a dark basemap for contrast">
    Heatmap colors stand out most clearly against a dark background. Ask MapX to switch the basemap:

    ```text
    Use dark basemap
    ```

    You can switch back at any time: *"Switch to satellite basemap"* or *"Use the light street map."*
  </Step>

  <Step title="Export or include in a report">
    Once you're happy with the visualization, you have two export paths:

    - **Export as PNG** — ask *"Export the current map view as PNG"* for a static image suitable for slides or documents
    - **Include in Report** — click **Export Report** to generate a full HTML report with the heatmap, data table, and AI insights embedded (see [Generating Reports](/en/guides/reports))
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Heatmap Prompts by Industry

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Retail">
    Retail analysts use heatmaps to understand where customer demand concentrates, which store locations underperform relative to their catchment, and where new locations would capture the most foot traffic.

    **Sample prompt:**
    ```text
    Create a heatmap weighted by annual_sales for each store location,
    then show me which ZIP codes have high density but low sales coverage
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Real Estate">
    Real estate professionals use heatmaps to visualize price gradients across a market, highlight high-demand neighborhoods, and show clients where value is appreciating fastest.

    **Sample prompt:**
    ```text
    Show a price heatmap for all listings, weighted by price_per_sqft,
    and switch to a dark basemap for the client presentation
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Public Sector">
    Government agencies and urban planners use density heatmaps to identify where service demand is highest, where incidents cluster, and where infrastructure investment will have the greatest impact.

    **Sample prompt:**
    ```text
    Create a density heatmap of 311 service requests over the last 6 months
    and highlight census tracts where request frequency exceeds 200 per km²
    ```
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
