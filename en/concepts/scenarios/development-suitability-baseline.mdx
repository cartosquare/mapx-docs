Development Suitability Baseline answers the first question of any growth plan: **where can this city build, and how much can it hold** once ecological, terrain and flood constraints are respected. It follows the two-part appraisal logic used in Chinese territorial planning — hard constraints first, suitability and carrying capacity second, boundary and tiered controls last.

![The Nairobi scenario as delivered: development control tiers drawn over ecological protection areas, protected areas and the 50 m riparian buffer](/images/scenarios/development-suitability-baseline.jpg)

## What the scenario ships

Opening the scenario imports eight prepared layers for the Nairobi window. Every layer comes from the [built-in datasets](/en/concepts/builtin-datasets) catalog with its default style, legend, source, licence and attribution attached.

| Layer | Data | Role in the study |
|---|---|---|
| Road network | 52,642 OSM lines | Existing urban skeleton; reference for the boundary and for infill versus new growth |
| Watercourses | 718 OSM lines | Source of the 50 m riparian buffer |
| Administrative boundaries | 26 ADM3 units (geoBoundaries) | Attribution, summary tables and map labels |
| Protected area constraints | 379 Overture features across Kenya | Part of the ecological exclusion |
| DEM | Copernicus DEM GLO-30, 30 m (1,459–1,954 m) | Slope classes |
| Land cover | Esri Sentinel-2 2020, 10 m, 9 classes (57% built-up) | Ecological classes and the existing built-up surface |
| Flood hazard | WRI Aqueduct, 1 km | Flood avoidance line |
| Population | GHS-POP, 1 km | Current population reference for the capacity comparison (hidden by default) |

The layer order is the display order: points, then lines, then polygons, then rasters. All eight stay in the project for follow-up questions; only the surfaces you see in the legend above are styled as deliverables.

## How the study works

The method is a chain of three planning questions. Each one maps onto deterministic [analysis](/en/concepts/analysis) tools, so every number in the report can be traced back to a run.

| Stage | Question | What MapX runs | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Screen the hard constraints | Which land is off-limits? | Slope, Reclassify, Buffer, Raster calculator, Overlay erase, Raster class area, Zonal statistics | Ecological protection mask and its polygons, the 50 m riparian buffer, constraint-area tables |
| 2. Grade and size what remains | How suitable is the remaining land, and how many people can it carry? | Weighted overlay (equal weights by default), Reclassify, Grid at 1 km, Zonal statistics | Suitability classes 1–4, population per evaluation cell, class-area and capacity tables |
| 3. Propose the boundary | Where should the development boundary run, and what tiers apply? | Raster to polygons, Overlay erase and dissolve, Zonal summary, Label anchors | Development control tiers (priority / managed / restricted), charts and a written report |

**The constraint definitions are explicit.** Ecological protection is the union of protected areas, the water, tree and flooded-vegetation land-cover classes, and the 50 m watercourse buffer; slope is split at 8°, 15° and 25°; flood is an avoidance line, not a suitability score. Suitability is then graded 1–4 over the land that survives those exclusions.

**The capacity estimate is a planning convention.** Carrying capacity is derived at 100 m² of urban construction land per person. The report states the assumption; it is an estimate, not a measured capacity.

## Platform capabilities

- **[Plan mode](/en/concepts/plan-mode)** turns the request into a reviewable plan: the AI asks about the study area, which constraints are hard, and the report audience before anything runs.
- **[Analysis tools](/en/concepts/analysis)** do the computation: slope and reclassification, raster calculator masks, buffering, weighted overlay, 1 km grids, zonal statistics and polygon conversion.
- **[Layer styling](/en/concepts/layers)** gives the control tiers human category labels; the constraint layers stay translucent so the tiers remain readable.
- **[Charts](/en/concepts/charts)** and a Word **[report](/en/concepts/reports)** package the class areas, the constraint breakdown and the capacity ranking for delivery.

## Run it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the scenario">
    Go to **Scenarios** in the top menu, select **Development Suitability Baseline** and choose **Open scenario**. Eight layers land in the project and the camera flies to Nairobi (36.82, -1.29).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send the suggested question">
    The scenario ships one starter prompt: *"Run a development-suitability study for Nairobi: screen the ecological, terrain and flood constraints, grade buildable land and carrying capacity, then propose a development boundary with tiered controls; deliver layers, charts and a report."* Edit it if your study area or constraints differ.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Answer the plan clarifications">
    Plan mode asks three things: the study area and admin unit, which constraints count as hard for this city, whether the ecological mask is a hard exclusion (the default) or a graded factor, and the report language. Everything else — grid size, slope thresholds, class breaks — is stated as an assumption.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review and approve the plan">
    Read the plan document, adjust any step, then approve it. Each step runs as a deterministic tool with its own status, time and result; the run graph shows how the layers feed each other.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Refine and deliver">
    Turn on the population layer to compare present-day population with the estimated capacity, restyle the tiers, then export the report or share the map.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Example output

<Note>The values below come from one example run with the default settings on the shipped data. They show the shape of the deliverables, not what your run will return — your window, parameters and data updates will change them.</Note>

In one example run on the shipped Nairobi data, the deliverables were a map with three development control tiers, the ecological protection polygons, protected areas and the riparian buffer, plus:

- four suitability classes of **0.4 / 4.2 / 446 / 83.6 km²** after hard exclusions;
- a constraint breakdown of **105.5 km²** protected areas, **40.4 km²** riparian buffer, **39.1 km²** ecological composite, **5 km²** flood avoidance area and **0.04 km²** steep slope (≥25°);
- a capacity ranking by sub-county — Kasarani, Langata and Embakasi East lead the estimate;
- three charts (buildable land by class, constraints screened, capacity by sub-county) and one report.

## Read the results with care

- **Flood is an avoidance constraint here.** The shipped 1 km flood raster has only a few valid cells in this window; treat it as a "keep clear of the floodplain" line. A graded flood factor needs a different dataset.
- **Capacity is an estimate at 100 m² per person.** Report it as a planning assumption, never as measured capacity.
- **Population never enters the suitability score.** It is a present-day reference for comparing what exists with what the land could hold.
- **The ecological mask is a product of its inputs.** It uses protected areas plus three land-cover classes and a 50 m buffer; grass and shrub are deliberately excluded, and local statutory boundaries should replace the country-wide preview when you have them.
- **Equal weights are the default composite.** If you want AHP or expert weights, run the weighted overlay as a documented sensitivity variant and compare the class tables.
