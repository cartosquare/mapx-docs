Carrying Capacity Assessment asks how much development a city can absorb before its water, climate and hazard constraints become the binding limit. For Jakarta — a low, flat delta city with frequent waterlogging — the study reads four constraints together, grades the result, and separates "constrained land" from "land under pressure".

![The Jakarta scenario as delivered: carrying-capacity classes 1–4, overload cells and the constraint-composition chart](/images/scenarios/carrying-capacity-assessment.jpg)

## What the scenario ships

Six prepared layers cover the Jakarta window. The rasters except rainfall start hidden so the class raster stays readable; turn them on when you want to inspect a single factor.

| Layer | Data | Role in the study |
|---|---|---|
| River network | OSM waterways | Drainage and flood corridor |
| Administrative boundaries | 46 kecamatan (OCHA COD-AB ADM3); 44 on the mainland | Study-area definition, attribution and district labels |
| Precipitation | CHIRPS, ~5.35 km | Rainfall membership (waterlogging constraint) |
| Population | WorldPop, 100 m (hidden) | Exposure and pressure axis |
| Flood hazard | WRI Aqueduct, ~900 m (hidden) | Flood-depth membership |
| Terrain | SRTM, 30 m (hidden) | Slope and topographic wetness |

All six come from the [built-in datasets](/en/concepts/builtin-datasets) catalog, with source, licence and attribution attached.

## How the study works

The design follows the "shortest stave" reading of carrying capacity: the most limiting factor decides the class, not the average.

| Stage | Question | What MapX runs | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Build the capacity classes | Which factor limits each place, and how strongly? | Slope, Topographic wetness index, Fuzzy membership, Raster calculator (`minimum`), Focal statistics repair, Euclidean distance, Reclassify | Four-class carrying-capacity raster with the 300 m drainage-corridor adjustment |
| 2. Diagnose pressure | Where is low capacity combined with high population? | Grid at 1 km, Zonal statistics, Quadrant (median split) | Overload cells, population per cell, district pressure table |
| 3. Explain and mitigate | Which constraint dominates, and what should each district do? | Raster class area, Zonal statistics, Charts, Report | Class-area and dominant-constraint tables, mounted pressure chart and report |

**Four memberships feed the composite.** Slope is scored from 0–25°, wetness from the TWI range calibrated inside the study window (6.08–9.28), flood depth from 0–3 m and rainfall from 1770–2521 mm inside the same window. Rainfall is a *waterlogging* constraint: more rain means a stronger limitation, not a bigger resource.

**Gaps are repaired, not hidden.** The flood raster stops at its model domain and the wetness index is undefined on perfectly flat cells. Both are filled from their own neighbourhood mean and reported as a data repair; a coverage floor of 99% of the study area is the acceptance check.

**Population is the pressure axis.** It enters the overload quadrant and the capacity estimate, never the suitability composite — a crowded district is not thereby an unsuitable one.

## Run it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the scenario">
    Choose **Carrying Capacity Assessment** in the scenario catalog. Six layers import and the camera flies to Jakarta (106.85, -6.21).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Send the suggested question">
    *"Run a resource-and-environment carrying-capacity study for Jakarta: assess how water, climate and flood constraints limit growth, flag overloaded areas, then recommend measures by dominant constraint; deliver layers, charts and a report."*
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm the method">
    The plan asks whether to read the main-city extent or the full administrative area including the offshore islands, whether to use the strict `minimum` composite (the default) or a compensating fuzzy gamma when a class degenerates, and which numbers the report should quote.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Approve and watch the run">
    After approval, each step runs as a deterministic analysis. The plan graph shows the four membership rasters feeding the limiting-factor composite and the pressure quadrant.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the failure modes in the result">
    Check that every class is non-empty and that the delivered raster covers at least 99% of the study area — that is the method's acceptance floor, not a promise about your numbers. If a class collapses, the documented fix is to calibrate the membership scale or run the gamma variant — not to renumber the classes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Example output

<Note>The values below come from one example run with the default settings on the shipped data. They show the shape of the deliverables, not what your run will return — your window, parameters and data updates will change them.</Note>

In one example run on the shipped Jakarta data, the deliverables were:

- **99.1%** coverage of the study area after repairing both input gaps (91.9% before the repair), with the NoData patches reduced from thousands of speckles to a small remainder;
- four non-empty classes of roughly **11 / 23 / 25 / 41%** of the study area, where class 4 is the most constrained;
- a dominant-constraint reading of **wetness 57%**, **rainfall 33%**, with flood depth and slope accounting for the remainder;
- overload cells bringing together low capacity and high population, plus the per-district pressure table;
- one report and a mounted constraint-composition chart.

The population column should total the study-area population within about ±10% of the published census figure; a much larger sum points to a unit or resolution bug rather than a finding.

## Read the results with care

- **Rainfall is a constraint, not a resource.** The scenario treats wetter as more limiting, following the waterlogging reading used in territorial appraisal.
- **Population is never a suitability factor.** It is exposure and pressure only.
- **The 300 m drainage corridor is an analysis default.** It degrades cells inside the corridor and must be labelled as an assumption.
- **Two gaps are repaired and disclosed.** Never impute zero — zero falsely asserts "no flood". The report names what was filled and how much area it affected.
- **Land subsidence is out of scope.** The catalog ships no subsidence dataset, so the report says "subsidence not modelled" instead of substituting another raster.
- **Rainfall resolution is coarse.** At ~5.35 km it is a background constraint; quote it as such.
