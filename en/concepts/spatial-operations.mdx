Spatial operations are the analytical building blocks of GIS — they answer questions like *"what's near what?"*, *"where do these two datasets overlap?"*, and *"which locations score best across multiple factors?"*. In traditional GIS tools, running these operations requires technical training and manual configuration. In MapX, you simply describe your goal in plain English and the platform selects, configures, and executes the right operation automatically.

The sections below describe every spatial operation MapX supports, when to reach for each one, and example prompts you can use or adapt. End-to-end workflows for common use cases are covered further down.

## Spatial Operations at a Glance

### Buffer Analysis

Buffer analysis creates a zone of a specified distance around each feature in a dataset — points, lines, or polygons. The result is a new geometry layer showing everything that falls within that radius.

**When to use it:** Catchment area mapping, proximity analysis, service-area planning, regulatory setback checks.

```text Example prompt
Show me all coffee shops within 800m of subway stations
```

```text Example prompt
Which residential parcels fall within 200m of the proposed highway extension?
```

### Spatial Join

A spatial join transfers attributes from one dataset onto features in another based on their geographic relationship (e.g., containment or intersection). The result enriches your existing data without requiring a manual key-based merge.

**When to use it:** Associating administrative boundaries (neighborhoods, ZIP codes, census tracts) with point datasets; tagging events with the zone they fall in; combining demographic data with location records.

```text Example prompt
Attach neighborhood names to each of my store locations
```

```text Example prompt
Join census tract income data to my customer points
```

### Heatmap

A heatmap visualizes the spatial distribution of point data as a smooth color gradient — high-density or high-value areas appear in warm colors, low-density areas in cool colors. MapX supports both count-based heatmaps and value-weighted heatmaps.

**When to use it:** Customer density mapping, incident frequency analysis, sales concentration, foot traffic visualization.

```text Example prompt
Create a heatmap of customer density across the city
```

```text Example prompt
Show a heatmap of delivery incidents weighted by severity score
```

### Overlay / Intersection

Overlay analysis finds the geographic areas where two datasets coincide. MapX can compute intersections (only the overlapping portions) and differences (areas in one layer but not another), producing new geometry representing the result.

**When to use it:** Identifying parcels within flood zones or protected areas; finding census blocks that intersect a proposed development footprint; combining zoning layers with land-use data.

```text Example prompt
Show which parcels in my dataset fall inside the flood risk zone
```

```text Example prompt
Find the overlap between my delivery zones and the city's commercial districts
```

### Site Scoring

Site scoring — also called multi-criteria analysis — evaluates a set of candidate locations against several weighted factors simultaneously and produces a ranked score for each. You define the criteria; MapX computes the composite scores and visualizes them on the map.

**When to use it:** Retail site selection, facility planning, logistics depot placement, competitive landscape analysis, franchise territory evaluation.

```text Example prompt
Score these 50 locations by population density, distance from competitors, and foot traffic
```

```text Example prompt
Rank my candidate sites based on proximity to highways, median household income, and available square footage
```

### Filtering & Styling

Filtering restricts the visible features to those matching an attribute condition (e.g., revenue above a threshold, category equals a specific value). Styling controls how those features are rendered — point size, color scale, opacity — based on column values.

**When to use it:** Highlighting revenue tiers, isolating a single product category, comparing performance across regions, preparing clean maps for reports or presentations.

```text Example prompt
Show only stores with revenue above $500k, color by region
```

```text Example prompt
Filter to locations opened after 2020 and size the points by monthly visitors
```

## End-to-End Workflows

### Creating Heatmaps

Rather than showing individual points, a heatmap reveals where things *concentrate* — surfacing hotspots, deserts, and gradients that would be invisible in a raw scatter plot. MapX generates heatmaps from any dataset through a single plain-English prompt, with no layer configuration, color ramp selection, or kernel tuning required.

#### Types of Heatmaps

A **density heatmap** shows where points cluster together, regardless of any attribute value — intensity is determined purely by how many points fall within a given radius. Use it to answer questions like *"Where do incidents occur most frequently?"* or *"Which neighborhoods have the highest concentration of listings?"*

A **weighted heatmap** uses a numeric column to modulate intensity — points in a dense cluster contribute more heat if their attribute values are high. Use it when the *magnitude* of an attribute matters, not just its presence: *"Which store locations generate the most revenue?"* or *"Where is property value highest per square meter?"* When you specify a column in your prompt, MapX automatically switches to weighted mode.

#### Creating a Heatmap

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload your dataset">
    Upload a CSV, GeoJSON, or Excel file containing your point data. MapX will auto-detect location columns (`lat`/`lng`, address strings, or geometry fields) and render the raw points on the map.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask for the heatmap">
    For a **density heatmap**, simply describe what you want visualized:

    ```text
    Show a heatmap of incident frequency across the city
    ```

    For a **weighted heatmap**, specify the column to use as intensity:

    ```text
    Create a heatmap weighted by revenue for each store location
    ```

    ```text
    Show a heatmap of property values using the price_per_sqm column
    ```
  </Step>

  <Step title="Adjust radius and intensity">
    Fine-tune the visual appearance with follow-up prompts:

    ```text
    Increase the heatmap radius to 2km
    ```

    ```text
    Reduce the blur radius — I want to see tighter, more distinct hotspots
    ```

    ```text
    Increase the intensity — the heatmap looks too faint
    ```

    You can also ask for specific color ramps: *"Use a blue-to-red color scale"* or *"Switch to a purple gradient."*
  </Step>

  <Step title="Switch to a dark basemap for contrast">
    Heatmap colors stand out most clearly against a dark background. Ask MapX to switch the basemap:

    ```text
    Use dark basemap
    ```

    You can switch back at any time: *"Use the light basemap"* or *"Use the light basemap with labels."*
  </Step>

  <Step title="Export or include in a report">
    Once you're happy with the visualization, you have two export paths:

    - **Export as PNG** — ask *"Export the current map view as PNG"* for a static image suitable for slides or documents
    - **Include in Report** — click **Export Report** to generate a full HTML report with the heatmap, data table, and AI insights embedded (see [Reports](/en/concepts/reports))
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Running a Site Selection Analysis

Site selection has traditionally required GIS specialists, days of data wrangling, and expensive software licenses. With MapX, upload your candidate locations, describe your scoring criteria in plain English, and receive a ranked, color-coded map with a full score breakdown in seconds.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload your candidate locations CSV">
    Prepare a CSV file with at minimum the columns `name`, `lat`, and `lng`. You can include any additional attributes you want to factor into scoring — store size, lease cost, foot traffic estimates, and so on.

    Upload the file by dragging it onto the MapX canvas or using the **Upload Data** button. MapX will auto-detect the location columns and plot your candidates on the map.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Upload or describe your reference datasets">
    Site scoring depends on reference layers — datasets you want to measure proximity to or overlap with. Upload any combination of:

    - **Competitor locations** (CSV with lat/lng)
    - **Transit stops** (CSV, GeoJSON, or a public dataset URL)
    - **Demographic areas** (GeoJSON polygons with population attributes)

    If you don't have a file, you can describe the data in chat and MapX will suggest public datasets or let you proceed with estimated values.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask MapX to score your locations">
    Type your scoring criteria directly into the AI chat. Be explicit about the metrics and weights you want:

    ```text
    Score my 50 candidate sites by: population density within 1km (weight 40%),
    distance from nearest competitor (weight 35%),
    distance from nearest transit stop (weight 25%)
    ```

    MapX will run buffer calculations, spatial joins, and weighted scoring automatically — no formulas or GIS commands needed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review the scored map and ranked list">
    MapX returns two outputs:

    - A **color-coded map layer** where each candidate site is styled by its composite score (green = highest, red = lowest by default)
    - A **ranked data table** in the chat panel showing each site's name, total score, and per-criterion breakdown

    Click any point on the map to see its individual scores and how it performed on each criterion.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Export the analysis as a report or share the map link">
    When you're satisfied with the results, you have two options:

    - **Export Report** — generates a professional HTML report containing the scored map, ranked table, and AI-written narrative summary (see [Reports](/en/concepts/reports))
    - **Share Map Link** — creates a public or password-protected link to the live interactive map

    Both options are available from the project toolbar in the top-right corner.
  </Step>
</Steps>

#### Refining the Scoring

Your first scoring pass is rarely your last. Use follow-up prompts to iterate without starting over:

```text
Increase the population density weight to 50% and reduce competitor distance to 25%
```

```text
Add a fourth criterion: distance from highway on-ramp (weight 15%) —
rebalance the other weights proportionally
```

```text
Remove sites with a score below 60 from the ranked list
```

```text
Re-score using only sites within the Chicago metro area
```

MapX maintains the full context of your project, so each follow-up builds on the previous result. You can also ask for plain-English explanations: *"Why did Site #12 score so low?"*

#### What's in the Output

After scoring, your project contains a **Scored Candidates** layer with the following attributes added to each point:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `composite_score` | Weighted total score (0–100) |
| `rank` | Ordinal rank across all candidates |
| `score_population_density` | Sub-score for the population criterion |
| `score_competitor_distance` | Sub-score for competitor proximity |
| `score_transit_distance` | Sub-score for transit accessibility |
| `nearest_competitor_m` | Actual distance in meters to closest competitor |
| `nearest_transit_m` | Actual distance in meters to closest transit stop |

The color ramp, score thresholds, and column names can all be adjusted through follow-up prompts.

### Logistics & Warehouse Coverage

Logistics teams use MapX to turn raw depot and customer data into actionable spatial intelligence — visualizing delivery zones, identifying coverage gaps, and evaluating new warehouse placements.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload your warehouse or depot locations">
    Prepare a CSV with at minimum the columns `name`, `lat`, and `lng`. Optional columns like `capacity`, `region`, or `fleet_size` can be referenced later in your analysis.

    Upload the file via **Upload Data** or drag it onto the map canvas. MapX will auto-detect the location columns and render your depots as a point layer.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Generate coverage buffer zones">
    Ask MapX to build delivery coverage zones around each warehouse:

    ```text
    Show delivery coverage zones of 50km around each warehouse
    ```

    MapX creates a buffer polygon for each depot and overlays them on the map. You can request multiple radii at once (e.g., inner 25km and outer 50km zones) or use drive-time estimates if routing data is available.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Upload your customer locations">
    Upload a second CSV containing your customer or order destination data. Include columns for `customer_id`, `lat`, `lng`, and any attributes relevant to prioritization — such as `order_volume` or `tier`.

    MapX will plot customers as a separate layer and keep both layers active for the spatial join step.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Identify customers outside coverage zones">
    Run a spatial join to find which customers fall outside your delivery network:

    ```text
    Which customers fall outside all coverage zones?
    ```

    MapX highlights the uncovered customers in a distinct color and returns a count and list in the chat. You can ask for a filtered export of just those records, or drill down by region.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Optimize warehouse placement">
    Once you know where the gaps are, ask MapX to suggest where to add capacity:

    ```text
    Where should I add a warehouse to minimize uncovered customers?
    ```

    MapX uses the spatial distribution of uncovered customers to recommend one or more candidate locations, ranked by the number of customers each placement would bring into coverage. You can iterate by specifying constraints: *"Suggest locations within 10km of a highway"* or *"Limit suggestions to these three metro areas."*
  </Step>
</Steps>

#### Example Prompts for Logistics

Use these prompts as starting points — adjust distances, column names, and thresholds to match your data:

```text

Which customers fall outside all coverage zones?

Where should I add a warehouse to minimize uncovered customers?

Color customers by which warehouse zone they fall in

Show me which warehouse serves the most customers

Highlight customers with order_volume > 100 who are outside coverage

```

#### Working with Large Datasets

If you're working with very large national or global datasets, a few tips will help you get faster, more focused results:

- **Filter by region first** — ask MapX to *"Show only customers in the Northeast region"* before running a spatial join, so operations run on a smaller subset
- **Use bounding box prompts** — *"Analyze only customers within 200km of Chicago"* scopes the computation geographically
- **Layer visibility** — toggle off layers you're not actively analyzing; MapX keeps them in memory but stops rendering them, which keeps the canvas responsive
- **Chunked uploads** — for very large datasets, split by region before uploading and analyze each chunk separately

### Real Estate Market Analysis

Real estate professionals use MapX to transform listing spreadsheets into compelling spatial stories — mapping properties, overlaying neighborhood context, comparing demographics across areas, and delivering polished client presentations.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload your property listings CSV">
    Prepare a CSV with your listings data. MapX works with either address strings or explicit coordinates:

    - Address-based: include a column like `address` or `full_address` — MapX will geocode automatically
    - Coordinate-based: include `lat` and `lng` columns for instant rendering

    Include attributes you'll want to filter or visualize: `price`, `bedrooms`, `sqft`, `property_type`, `neighborhood`, `days_on_market`, and so on.

    Upload via **Upload Data** or drag the file onto the canvas. MapX will detect and plot all listings within seconds.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Generate a price heatmap">
    Get an immediate read on where values are concentrated:

    ```text
    Create a price heatmap for these properties
    ```

    MapX generates a weighted heatmap using the `price` column as intensity. Hot zones indicate high-value clusters; cooler areas show where lower-priced inventory concentrates. You can follow up to adjust radius, switch color ramps, or normalize by square footage.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add neighborhood context layers">
    Layer in the amenities and services that drive buyer decisions:

    ```text
    Show schools within 1km of each property
    ```

    ```text
    Add parks and green spaces within 500m
    ```

    ```text
    Show flood zone boundaries overlaid on my listings
    ```

    MapX fetches or accepts uploaded reference datasets and performs the spatial join, annotating each property with proximity data you can then filter on.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Filter and compare specific segments">
    Drill into the inventory most relevant to your client:

    ```text
    Show only 3-bedroom properties priced under $800k, color by neighborhood
    ```

    MapX applies the filter and re-styles the layer in one step. You can stack multiple filters, switch the color dimension, or ask for a side-by-side comparison of two price brackets.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Export a client-ready report">
    When your analysis is complete, generate a professional deliverable:

    - Click **Export Report** in the project toolbar
    - Choose which sections to include: the price heatmap, filtered listings map, neighborhood comparison charts, and AI-written market summary
    - Download as HTML or share a direct link

    The report is ready to send to your client without any additional formatting. See [Reports](/en/concepts/reports) for full details.
  </Step>
</Steps>
